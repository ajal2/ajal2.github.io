**Won Best Paper for a cost-variance analysis at a precision machine shop.**

- **Situation:** A contract CNC shop outside Chicago, 85 machines, aerospace and defense parts. Jobs are quoted at a fixed price, so every cost overrun comes straight out of margin. Their ERP was from the 1990s and cost reports ran a month behind. They found out after the part had shipped.
- **What I did:** I owned the economics. Sized the leak at $1.44M a year: variance on every job, rolled up by part, counting only the parts that ran over. Netted out it looked like $600K, because the underruns were hiding the overruns. The team built the Power BI dashboard on the legacy ERP, daily views instead of month-old Excel.
- **Value:** The business case was deliberately conservative. I anchored it to $323K of savings the shop had already proven, not to the $1.44M leak, and it still paid back in about 8 months. Won Best Paper at a 41-team national design competition.
- **What I learned:** Netting nearly buried the finding. Underruns were cancelling out the overruns, and the full $1.44M only showed up once I worked the variance part by part.
